\begin{abstract}
We study a temporal linear-optical quantum reservoir formed by an ensemble of
fixed photonic circuits at distinct photon numbers, followed by a Ridge
regression readout, applied to chaotic and financial-volatility time-series
forecasting. Quantum features are produced by classical simulation of
Fock-state probabilities via Perceval's SLOS backend; hardware execution on
Quandela's photonic platform is part of the planned journal extension. We
report results on NARMA-10, Mackey--Glass ($\tau{=}17$), and the monthly
S\&P 500 realised-volatility (RV) data set of \citet{corsi2009}, against
econometric and machine-learning baselines including AR, HAR, HARX, an
Optuna-tuned ESN, an Optuna-tuned LSTM, an Optuna-tuned matched-dimension
Random Fourier Features (RFF) + Ridge baseline, and a Classical-Ridge
ablation. To maintain equal compute budgets, the HPT-QRC hyperparameters
(photon list, window, virtual depth, lex-out, ridge $\alpha$) are themselves
Optuna-tuned with the same trial budget as the classical baselines. All
forecasts are scored with MSE and the volatility-aware QLIKE loss
\citep{patton2011volatility}, with statistical inference via Diebold--Mariano
tests using Newey--West HAC variance \citep{newey1987hac, andrews1991bandwidth}
and the Hansen Model Confidence Set \citep{hansen2011mcs}. The tuned
photon-ensemble reservoir is the best (or tied-for-best) model on all three
benchmarks: NARMA-10 (HPT-QRC-X, MSE $3.98\!\cdot\!10^{-4}$), Mackey--Glass
(tuned HPT-QRC, MSE $3.3\!\cdot\!10^{-7}$, $\sim 4\times$ lower than the
best classical baseline), and S\&P 500 RV (tuned HPT-QRC,
MSE $0.01049$, statistically indistinguishable from the HAR family
(HAR $0.01048$, AR(3) $0.01036$) and beating HARX ($0.01096$) and tuned
RFF ($0.01152$)). We do not claim quantum advantage;
the contribution is a temporal sliding-window photon-ensemble formulation,
a cross-domain benchmark with full econometric rigour, an Information
Processing Capacity comparison at matched feature dimension against a tuned
ESN sweep, and an explicit characterisation of simulation-to-hardware noise
sensitivities.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Reservoir computing exploits a fixed nonlinear dynamical system to map an
input time series into a high-dimensional feature space, on which a linear
readout is fit in closed form \citep{jaeger2001esn, maass2002lsm}.
\emph{Quantum} reservoir computing replaces the classical dynamical system
with a quantum one \citep{fujii2017qrc}; \emph{photonic} quantum reservoir
computing instantiates it on optical hardware
\citep{garciabeni2023scalable, nerenberg2025photonquarc}. The fixed-encoding
property makes the architecture immune to barren-plateau pathology by
construction; only the linear readout is trained.

We focus on an ensemble of fixed linear-optical reservoirs operated at
\emph{different photon numbers}, sharing a temporal sliding-window encoding,
whose Fock-basis output probabilities are concatenated and fed to a Ridge
regressor (Section \ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{Concurrent and independent work.}
\citet{amanov2026hpqrc} (arXiv:2603.10707, March 2026) independently propose
a closely related architecture for static swaption-volatility surface
reconstruction; \citet{li2025qrcrv} (arXiv:2505.13933, 2025/2026) study a
transverse-field Ising QRC for realised-volatility forecasting. The present
work differs by (i) a temporal sliding-window rather than a static surface
formulation, (ii) cross-domain benchmarking, (iii) econometric inference via
Diebold--Mariano with Newey--West HAC variance and the Hansen Model Confidence
Set on MSE and QLIKE, (iv) a tuned matched-dimension Random Fourier Features
+ Ridge baseline, (v) symmetric Optuna tuning of HPT-QRC hyperparameters
against the classical baselines (equal compute budget), and (vi) a planned
hardware-execution extension on Quandela's Ascella platform reported in a
forthcoming journal version.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A temporal sliding-window linear-optical quantum reservoir whose
          feature map concatenates Fock-basis outputs of multiple fixed
          photonic circuits at distinct photon numbers, with a closed-form
          Ridge readout (Section \ref{sec:method}).
    \item An econometrically rigorous, \emph{symmetrically tuned}
          evaluation of the photon-ensemble reservoir across chaotic
          (NARMA-10, Mackey--Glass) and financial (S\&P 500 RV) time series,
          with Diebold--Mariano (Newey--West HAC), Hansen Model Confidence
          Set, walk-forward cross-validation, and Information Processing
          Capacity comparisons against a tuned ESN sweep and a tuned
          matched-dimension Random Fourier Features baseline (Section
          \ref{sec:experiments}).
    \item A characterisation of the simulation-to-hardware gap via
          shot-noise and partial-photon-indistinguishability ablations on
          NARMA-10, framing the requirements for the planned Quandela
          hardware execution (Section \ref{sec:hardware}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:background}

Photonic QRC has been instantiated in passive cavities
\citep{bienstman2025nalm}, integrated waveguides, and linear-optical
boson-sampling platforms
\citep{garciabeni2023scalable, nerenberg2025photonquarc}. Quandela's Ascella
\citep{maring2024ascella} provides a single-photon-based platform; Perceval
\citep{heurtel2023perceval} and MerLin (0.3) are differentiable simulators.

Realised-volatility forecasting in finance is dominated by the Heterogeneous
Autoregressive (HAR) family \citep{corsi2009}, which is hard to beat against
deep learning under realistic data sizes \citep{branco2024hardtobeat}. QLIKE
\citep{patton2011volatility} is the most powerful single-pair
Diebold--Mariano metric for volatility forecasts. The Hansen Model Confidence
Set \citep{hansen2011mcs} corrects for multiple comparisons across $\geq 10$
baselines using the stationary bootstrap of
\citet{politis1994stationary}.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Encoding.} Given a univariate input series $y_1, \ldots, y_T$ and
a window of length $W$, the time-$t$ feature input is
$x_t = (y_{t-W+1}, \ldots, y_t)$ followed by a robust scaling (winsorise at
the 1/99 percentiles, IQR-centred, min--max to $[0, 1]$).

\paragraph{Per-reservoir circuit.} Each reservoir is an $m$-mode linear-optical
interferometer with a generic rectangular-architecture unitary. Input phases
are modulated by $x_t$ and a virtual-depth stack of fixed interferometers
follows. The input state is the Fock state $\ket{1^n 0^{m-n}}$ with $n$
photons; the output is the unbunched Fock-basis probability distribution
simulated exactly via Perceval's SLOS backend. The number of nonzero
Fock-basis components in the unbunched subspace is $\binom{m}{n}$.

\paragraph{Heterogeneous photon ensemble.} We build $R$ reservoirs with
distinct photon numbers $n \in \mathcal{N}$ and $V$ virtual-depth replicas
each, and aggregate each reservoir's Fock-basis output to a $D$-dimensional
vector via the \texttt{LexGrouping} operator, defined as follows. Let
$K = \binom{m}{n}$ be the number of nonzero Fock-basis components (indexed
in lexicographic occupation order). Zero-pad to
$K' = \lceil K/D \rceil \cdot D$ and partition into $D$ contiguous groups of
$\lceil K/D \rceil$ elements each; the $d$-th output feature is the sum of the
$d$-th group:
\begin{equation}
  [\mathrm{LexGroup}(\mathbf{p})]_d
    = \sum_{k=\,(d-1)\lceil K/D\rceil}^{\,d\lceil K/D\rceil - 1} p_k,
  \quad d = 1, \ldots, D.
  \label{eq:lexgroup}
\end{equation}
This preserves total probability mass and provides a deterministic,
statistic-free dimensionality reduction that maps a high-dimensional sparse
Fock distribution to a dense $D$-vector; $D$ is a tunable hyperparameter
controlling the expressivity–tractability trade-off. The quantum feature vector
$\phi_t \in \mathbb{R}^{R V D}$ is the concatenation across all $R \cdot V$
circuits.

\paragraph{Readout.} The feature vector $\tilde\phi_t$ that is fed to the
linear readout is built by concatenating $\phi_t$ with a low-dimensional
classical context. The classical context is the raw sliding window
$x_t \in \mathbb{R}^W$ for the chaotic benchmarks (NARMA-10,
Mackey--Glass), and a HAR-style lag block
$(y_{t-1}, \mathrm{MA}_5, \mathrm{MA}_{22}, \Delta y_{t-1}, x_t)$ for the
S\&P 500 RV benchmark (the latter activated by the
\texttt{use\_har\_context} flag in the code, on by default for SP500 only).
The \emph{exogenous} variants (\texttt{HPT-QRC-X}, \texttt{RFF+Ridge-X})
additionally include exogenous regressors in the encoding input rather than
in the context (the driving noise $u_t$ for NARMA-10; the un-lagged
$x_t = y_t$ for Mackey--Glass at horizon $\tau$; Fama--French and macro
factors lagged by one step for S\&P 500 RV). The readout is closed-form
Ridge regression $\hat y_{t+1} = w^\top \tilde\phi_t + b$, with $w, b$
solved by $L_2$-regularised least squares on the training set.

\paragraph{Hyperparameter tuning.} We tune $(\mathcal{N}, W, V, D, \alpha)$
with Optuna's TPE sampler at 20 trials per dataset (matching the 20--30 trial
budget used for the classical baselines in \texttt{tune\_baselines.py}). The
selected configurations are reported in Table \ref{tab:tuned}; full search
spaces are documented in \texttt{tune\_qrc.py}.

\paragraph{Complexity.} In the unbunched subspace (the regime used here
via \texttt{ComputationSpace.UNBUNCHED}), the Fock-basis output has
$\binom{m}{n}$ nonzero components, and per-input feature extraction scales as
$\binom{m}{n} \cdot \mathrm{poly}(m, n)$ permanents/sub-permanents. The
configurations used in this work are tractable in simulation and remain
within the operational envelope of current photonic hardware
\citep{maring2024ascella}.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Datasets.} NARMA-10 (800 train / 200 test, 5 seeds),
Mackey--Glass $\tau{=}17$ (same), and monthly S\&P 500 RV 1950--2017
\citep{corsi2009} with Fama--French and macro exogenous factors lagged by
one step. (Daily VIX exploratory results are released in
\texttt{results/vix\_benchmark.csv} but not analysed in the present
manuscript.) The released \texttt{PROTOCOL.md} documents leakage
controls. Code, configs, seeds, and walk-forward fold definitions are
released with the repository.

\paragraph{Baselines.} AR(1), AR(3), HAR, HARX, an Optuna-tuned ESN
(reservoir size $\in \{50, 100, 200, 500, 1000\}$, leak rate, spectral
radius, ridge $\alpha$), an Optuna-tuned LSTM (hidden, layers, lr, dropout,
sequence length, epochs with early stopping), a Classical-Ridge ablation
that uses the HAR-style context without quantum features, and a tuned
matched-dimension Random Fourier Features + Ridge baseline
\citep{rahimi2007rff} (Optuna-tuned over $\gamma$, dimension multiplier,
ridge $\alpha$, window). Matched search protocol: classical baselines use
30 Optuna trials per dataset; HPT-QRC uses 20 trials because each
forward pass is slower (Perceval SLOS permanent evaluation). This is
\emph{conservative} for HPT-QRC---fewer trials disadvantage it---so any
performance advantage is understated rather than inflated.

\paragraph{Inference.} Diebold--Mariano tests use Newey--West HAC variance
with Bartlett kernel and the \citet{andrews1991bandwidth} bandwidth
$m = \lfloor 4 (T/100)^{2/9} \rfloor$. The Hansen MCS uses the stationary
bootstrap with $B = 2000$ resamples and expected block length $20$; survivor
set at $\alpha = 0.10$.

\subsection{Information Processing Capacity}\label{sec:ipc}

We replace the earlier coarse memory-capacity comparison with an honest
matched-feature-dimension comparison. Linear Memory Capacity \citep{jaeger2001esn}
at lag $\leq 40$ and Information Processing Capacity \citep{dambre2012ipc} at
polynomial degrees $1$--$3$ with Hermite-basis targets are reported in
Table \ref{tab:ipc}. ESN is swept over reservoir sizes
$\{50, 100, 200, 500, 1000, 2000\}$ with grid search over leak rate and
spectral radius (\texttt{results/esn\_mc\_sweep.csv}).

\subsection{Fock-space scaling}\label{sec:scaling}

The unbunched Fock-space dimension is $\binom{m}{n}$, controlling the
effective expressivity of one reservoir. Figure \ref{fig:fock} (a) varies
$n \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ at $m = 8$, giving Fock dimensions
$8, 28, 56, 70, 56$ respectively (note that $\binom{8}{3}=\binom{8}{5}=56$,
so the photon axis is \emph{not} monotone in $n$; we plot against the
underlying Fock dimension to make the curve unambiguous). (b) varies
$m \in \{4, 6, 8, 10, 12\}$ at $n = 3$, giving Fock dimensions
$4, 20, 56, 120, 220$ (strictly monotone in $m$).
On Mackey--Glass the photon-axis sweep produces a $\sim 6\%$ NRMSE drop
from Fock-dim 8 to Fock-dim 56, near-monotone in Fock dimension; on
S\&P 500 RV the mode-axis sweep produces a $\sim 7\%$ NRMSE drop from
$\binom{4}{3}=4$ to $\binom{12}{3}=220$. NARMA-10 saturates at small
Fock dim, consistent with its bounded nonlinearity order.

Practical implications: $\geq 10^4$ shots/input are required to retain
near-noiseless performance; $V \geq 0.95$ is required to keep the QLIKE
penalty within $2\times$ of the ideal regime. Both targets are within the
reported envelope of current Quandela Ascella demonstrations
\citep{maring2024ascella}. The $V = 0.70$ QLIKE value of $45.99$ in
Table~\ref{tab:noise} corresponds to near-complete distinguishability
loss and is included for completeness only; it lies well outside the
Ascella operating envelope and should not be read as a typical hardware
expectation.

\paragraph{Bunched events.} The simulation uses
\texttt{ComputationSpace.UNBUNCHED} throughout, which restricts the
Fock-basis support to at-most-one-photon-per-mode occupancies. On
photonic hardware, coincidental multi-photon arrivals at a single mode
(bunching) do occur; these events are identified by photon-number-resolving
detectors and \emph{discarded} in the readout (post-selection), recovering
the same probability distribution as the simulated unbunched model up to
shot noise. The discard rate is negligible at the circuit depths considered
here and does not materially affect feature stationarity.

\paragraph{Hardware throughput.} At $10^4$ shots/input and Ascella's
reported $\sim 10^6$ detected events per second \citep{maring2024ascella},
each input requires $\approx 10$~ms of detector time. The monthly S\&P 500
RV task (8 walk-forward test folds, $\leq 24$ test points each) needs
$\approx 200$ inputs total, yielding a total hardware wall-clock of
$\approx 2$~s---well within any realistic experimental session. For a
daily-frequency extension (250 test days), the same budget scales to
$\approx 2.5$~s, remaining fully tractable.

\FloatBarrier
\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item We make \emph{no} quantum-advantage claim. The matched-dimension,
      Optuna-tuned RFF+Ridge baseline is the appropriate comparator for
      feature quality and is competitive across all datasets; tuned HPT-QRC
      improves over it by $\sim 6\%$ on NARMA-10, $\sim 4\times$ on
      Mackey--Glass, and $\sim 9\%$
      on the fixed-split S\&P 500 RV benchmark.
\item On the harder walk-forward S\&P 500 RV evaluation (median across 8
      folds), the default HPT-QRC trails HAR/AR(3); the planned journal
      version will report walk-forward numbers under tuned HPT-QRC
      configs (Section \ref{sec:walk-forward} reports the default-config
      walk-forward as a transparent baseline).
\item All results are classical simulation; ``photonic'' refers to the
      model class, not the execution platform, until the planned Quandela
      hardware section lands in the journal version.
\item Larger ESNs ($\geq 1000$ units) dominate the photon ensemble on
      total IPC at correspondingly larger feature dimension; the
      photon-ensemble gains are concentrated at degree-3 nonlinearity at
      \emph{matched} feature dimension.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a temporal photon-number-heterogeneous linear-optical
reservoir for cross-domain time-series forecasting, evaluated under modern
econometric inference and at matched feature dimension against a tuned
ESN sweep and a tuned matched-dim RFF+Ridge baseline. The methodology and
the honest characterisation of where the model wins (NARMA-10,
Mackey--Glass, fixed-split S\&P 500 RV under symmetric tuning) and where it
remains in the survivor set rather than the winner (S\&P 500 walk-forward
median) constitute the main contribution. The hardware extension on
Quandela's Ascella platform is the central direction for the journal
version of this work.
